Where FFOnto can be used, what it cannot do yet, who helped build it, and how to cite it.

Applications & Use Cases

Incident situational awareness
Combine what the drone images show, what the microphones hear and what the weather implies for one fire event, and see hazards with their recommended actions in one place.
Firefighter safety checks
Compare the protective equipment seen in an image with the equipment each role requires, and flag what is missing.
Model monitoring
Because model outputs are stored as observations with thresholds and provenance, the graph shows where classifiers, captioners and audio taggers disagree. These cases are useful for reviewing models and data.
Fire-weather analysis
Query FWI components and their trend by site and date, and see which operational conditions the danger rules imply.
Explainable question answering
FFOnto-Bot shows how an ontology can keep a language model's answers tied to evidence. It can serve as a baseline for research on knowledge-graph question answering.
Post-fire assessment and teaching
Burn evidence, ecosystem impacts and restoration measures in the vocabulary support damage assessment and teaching about fire ecology.

Limitations & Future Work

LimitationPlanned work
The danger-rule thresholds are placeholders. Replace them with values from published fire-danger classifications, each with its source.
Scene facts are only as good as the vision-language model that extracts them. Measure extraction precision and recall on a hand-annotated subset, and keep human validation flags.
Spatial relations such as near are judged from the image, not measured. Add geometry with GeoSPARQL where coordinates are available.
Many records lack capture time and coordinates, which limits linking across modalities. Use the SHACL reports to complete metadata at capture time.
Confidence of individual scene entities is not stored yet. Model scene attributes as observations with confidence, like classifier scores.
The assistant answers each message on its own. Support follow-up questions in a conversation.
The end-to-end evaluation of the assistant is preliminary. Evaluate on all held-out ForestFire-VQA questions once scene graphs exist for every image, and compare with a vision-language model answering directly from the image.

Acknowledgements

FFOnto was developed by Charuni Kosala Jayaweera under the supervision of Prof. Dulani Meedeniya.

The work builds on open standards and tools: the W3C SOSA, PROV-O, SKOS and SHACL recommendations and DCMI Metadata Terms. It also builds on the ROBOT tool and the HermiT reasoner for validation, the OOPS! pitfall scanner, WIDOCO for the ontology documentation, GraphDB for storage, and Ollama with open Qwen models for the assistant. The weather data come from the public Forest Fires (Montesinho) and Algerian Forest Fires datasets in the UCI Machine Learning Repository.
