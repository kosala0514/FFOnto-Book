FFOnto integrates three modalities. This chapter describes where each comes from, what a raw record looks like, and how its fields become ontology terms.

All three are lifted the same way. A normaliser cleans the raw record, the JSON-LD context maps each field to an ontology term, and records that share an event_id are linked to the same ffonto:FireEvent. That shared event is what makes cross-modal questions possible, such as whether the audio and the image of one incident agree.

Weather Data

Weather records are rows of a table that combines daily weather observations with the components of the Canadian Fire Weather Index (FWI) system. Two public datasets provide the rows:

  • Forest Fires (Montesinho natural park, Portugal). 517 records with a 9 × 9 spatial grid position, month and weekday, weather values, FWI components and the burned area (Cortez and Morais, 2007). The records carry no year or day of month, so they enter the graph without an observation date.
  • Algerian Forest Fires (Bejaia and Sidi Bel-Abbès regions). Daily records from June to September 2012 with full dates, weather values, all six FWI components and a fire / no-fire class.

Both are harmonised into one schema:

Two rules work on weather records. R04 compares each value with the danger rules and derives conditions such as rapid fine-fuel spread. R08 links each record to the previous record at the same site, so the FWI trend can be queried.

Image Data

Images are drone and camera frames from the ForestFire-VQA dataset. Each image has a JSON record produced by the image pipeline:

  • a caption from an image-captioning model (BLIP, large);
  • zero-shot scores for smoke, fire and no fire from CLIP ViT-L/14;
  • the decision thresholds in use;
  • file provenance and a SHA-256 checksum.

In this example, the smoke score (0.73) is above its threshold (0.60) and the fire score (0.16) is below its threshold (0.55), so rule R02 derives smoke as the detected class. The caption still says "forest fire", and rule R05 records that disagreement as an ffonto:ModalityConflict.

Questions about what is in a picture (people, PPE, vehicles, vegetation, damage) need more than scores. A vision-language model reads each image and writes a scene description: a list of entities with their kind, state, activity, worn equipment and spatial relations, restricted to concepts from the vocabulary. The lifter turns it into an ffonto:SceneDescription that ffonto:depicts a set of ffonto:SceneEntity nodes.

Audio Data

Audio clips are recorded at or near the fire. Each clip is classified with YAMNet, a sound-event model with 521 classes. YAMNet scores the clip in frames of 0.48 seconds, and the pipeline keeps the labels that reach a frame threshold of 0.2 and maps them to fire-relevant sound events:

Audio matters most in combination. When an explosion is detected above its threshold, rule R07 derives an explosion hazard for the event. Independently, rule R06 derives the same hazard type when a propane tank is seen near the fire in an image of that event. Both hazards share the hazard type ffontov:ExplosionHazard, and the vocabulary supplies its recommended actions: cool and isolate the pressurised tank, and withdraw to a safety zone.
